# Counts hold, but the votthapana story breaks

The four reference sheets are **roughly correct, and on numbers they are close to flawless**. Every headline count checked against Nārada's translation of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha and Bhikkhu Bodhi's *Comprehensive Manual* (CMA) matches. That covers 89/121, the 54/15/12/8 plane split, 52 cetasikas as 7/6/14/25, every sampayoga range (55, 66, 78, 73, 51, 69, 59, 16, 28, 47), every saṅgaha total from 38 down to 7, the 11 unfixed factors, the feeling totals (62 somanassa / 55 upekkhā on the 121 reckoning), and the 17-moment five-door process. The errors sit in definitions and rationales, and they cluster in one place: the account of **votthapana** (determining) in `vithi.md`. The sheet says votthapana determines the object "with greed" or "with generosity" (it is a rootless citta and has no greed or generosity in it). It says the mind-door adverting citta "performs adverting and determining in a single moment" (the determining function does not occur at the mind door, and a citta performs one function at a time). It also says kammic character is "settled" at votthapana, whereas the sources place the arising of wholesome and unwholesome in the **javana**, tipped by wise or unwise attention. `AGENTS.md` repeats the "settled" claim twice (lines 36 and 78) and builds its default practice leverage point on it. That matters more than any other finding here, because it governs how every scenario gets deconstructed. One other structural error stands out: `citta.md` line 123 calls the great resultants "identical in composition" to the great wholesome cittas, but they lack the abstinences and the illimitables. Below that come a few overstatements and many minor imprecisions. All of them can be fixed in a sentence each, and none requires changing a count.

The checks rely on four primary texts: Nārada's *Manual of Abhidhamma*, chapters I–V and IX (full text online); Bodhi's CMA (a partial PDF that is missing most of Ch. IV §7 onward and Ch. V); Rewata Dhamma's commentary on CMA IV–V; and Nina van Gorkom's books. The Visuddhimagga PDF could not be text-extracted, so Visuddhimagga and Atthasālinī definitions are cited through CMA, which reproduces them, and through van Gorkom's paragraph-numbered quotations. Line numbers refer to the current files in `/Users/yashpatil/Code/abhidharma/`.

## Votthapana is rootless, so it cannot settle anything with greed

These are the most serious problems in the repo, because they sit on the claim that `AGENTS.md` makes the default leverage point for practice.

**Error 1: `vithi.md` L62, "If the mind has determined the object with greed, greed-rooted cittas run; if with generosity, wholesome ones."** Votthapana is "the same type of citta—a rootless functional consciousness accompanied by equanimity" ([CMA III §9 Guide](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). It has no lobha and no alobha, and its cetanā accumulates no kamma. Greed and generosity are javana roots. Van Gorkom states the relation correctly: votthapana is "followed by akusala cittas or by kusala cittas. It depends on one's accumulations of akusala and kusala whether the votthapana citta will be succeeded by akusala cittas or by kusala cittas" ([van Gorkom, *Abhidhamma in Daily Life*](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/abhidhamma-in-daily-life/d/doc2714.html)). Correct version: *votthapana defines the object; the javanas that follow are wholesome or unwholesome according to wise or unwise attention and accumulated tendencies, and functional for an arahant.*

**Error 2: `vithi.md` L93, "the mind-door adverting citta performs adverting and determining in a single moment."** In a bare mind-door process, receiving, investigating and determining "do not occur; rather, mind-door adverting follows immediately upon the cutting off of the bhavaṅga," and "a consciousness can perform only one function at a time" ([CMA III §8–9 Guides](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). The sheet confused position with function. The mind-door adverting citta does occupy the slot just before the javanas, the same slot votthapana occupies at the five doors ("In a mind-door process the javana phase follows the mind-door adverting consciousness," CMA III endnote 8). But it performs only adverting. Correct version: *at the mind door there is no determining function; the mind-door adverting citta (the citta-type that does determining at the five doors) adverts, and the javanas follow.* `AGENTS.md` L36, "the mind door has no separate votthapana moment," is correct as worded. Its instruction to "find it by function" needs a corollary, though: at the mind door that function is absent, and the hinge is adverting.

**Overstatement 3: `vithi.md` L58 ("the point at which the kammic character of what follows is settled"), repeated in `AGENTS.md` L36 ("where the kammic character of the javanas is settled") and L78 ("The determining function settles the javanas' character").** None of the retrieved sources says this. Bodhi places the decisive point at javana: "The javana stage is the most important from an ethical standpoint, for it is at this point that wholesome or unwholesome cittas originate" ([CMA III §8 Guide](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). The tipping condition is attention: "If a worldling or a trainee applies wise attention (yoniso manasikāra), wholesome javanas will arise. If unwise attention is applied, unwholesome javanas will arise" ([Rewata Dhamma, paraphrasing CMA IV §11 Guide](https://Abhidhamma.com/Process_of_consciousness_and_matter.pdf)). One recent paper frames votthapana as conditioning "moral posture rather than producing moral action, while javana remains the locus of karmically productive volition" ([RSIS IJRISS](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/uploads/vol10-iss17-pg786-796-202609_pdf.pdf)). The strongest case for the repo's emphasis is the Atthasālinī's threefold manasikāra. That scheme reportedly names the adverting citta as the "javana-directing" kind of attention, but **it was not retrieved**, so the link stays unverified. The practice advice survives with an honest rewording: *votthapana is the last kammically inert moment; wholesome or unwholesome first arises in the javanas that follow; wise or unwise attention tips them; a pause works at this hinge.* That rewording keeps "pause before reaction" as the default move without claiming the hinge does the deciding.

Related to these, `vithi.md` L9 calls everything before the javanas "passive reception." Adverting and determining are functional (kiriya), not resultant, and the determining consciousness is one of the cittas that can produce bodily and vocal intimation ([CMA VI §11](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). They make no kamma, but "passive" is the wrong word for them.

## Composition errors make resultants look like they can hold right speech

**Error 4: `citta.md` L123, "identical in composition but passive."** The great resultants exclude the three abstinences and the two illimitables. That is why their maxima are 33/32/32/31, against 38/37/37/36 for the great wholesome cittas. In Nārada's words, "The Abstinences are absolutely moral. Hence they do not arise in a resultant consciousness" ([Nārada II](https://budsas.net/ebud/abhisgho/abhis02.htm)). The sheet itself gives these lower totals in `sampayoga.md` L131–144, so the two sheets contradict each other. What the two sets of eight share is the structure of feeling, knowledge, and prompting. A beginner reading L123 would conclude that a resultant moment can contain sammā vācā or karuṇā, and it cannot. The same imprecision appears in L106, which calls abstinences and illimitables "occasional adjuncts" of all 24 sobhana kāmāvacara. That holds only for mahākusala. Mahākiriya lacks the abstinences, mahāvipāka lacks both, and in lokuttara the abstinences are fixed and present together. L127 says of the arahant's mahākiriya that "the generosity, the kindness… are all still there." That is true, but the abstinences are gone, because arahants "do not need to deliberately refrain" ([CMA Ch. II](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)).

**Internal inconsistency 5: `sampayoga.md` L144 and L187, the reason great resultants lack the illimitables.** The sheet says a sense-sphere resultant "takes the object handed to it by past kamma rather than a being." Fine-material resultants are also produced by past kamma, and the sheet counts them inside karuṇā/muditā's 28. The textual reason is object type: sense-sphere resultants "take solely sense-sphere phenomena as their object, while the illimitables take the concept of beings as their object" ([CMA Ch. II §23 Guide](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). L144's "a sense-sphere result abstains from nothing" is compatible with the source but wordier than it. The source reason is simply that the abstinences are exclusively wholesome.

**Error 6 (small): `vithi.md` L54, santīraṇa "is where the desirability of the object is registered."** It is true that santīraṇa is the only rootless resultant with a joyful (somanassa) variety, which arises for an exceptionally desirable object ([Rewata Dhamma](https://Abhidhamma.com/Process_of_consciousness_and_matter.pdf)). But desirability is not first registered there. Eye-consciousness onward is already wholesome-resultant or unwholesome-resultant according to the object, and body-consciousness with sukha is a rootless resultant with pleasant feeling ([CMA III §9 Guide](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). Correct version: *santīraṇa is the only rootless resultant that can be accompanied by joy.*

**Misleading omission 7: `cetasika.md` L83, diṭṭhi.** The entry says the grasping that wrong view justifies "is greed, which is in all eight greed-rooted cittas," but never says that **diṭṭhi itself is in only four** of the eight. Māna occupies the other four, and only optionally ([CMA Ch. II §13](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). More broadly, cetasika.md drops the sub-groupings the counts depend on: aññasamāna 13; akusala 4 universal, then 3 in greed cittas, 4 in hate cittas, 2 in prompted cittas, and 1 in the doubt citta; sobhana 19 universal, then 3 abstinences, 2 illimitables, and 1 wisdom. Without them, a reader of that sheet alone cannot tell which beautiful factors are in every beautiful citta.

**Collapsed distinction 8: tatramajjhattatā rendered "Equanimity" (`cetasika.md` L143; `sampayoga.md` L88).** Bodhi defines it as "a mental attitude of balance," explicitly "not as neutral feeling," and translates it "neutrality of mind" ([CMA pp. 86–87](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). The sheets also use "equanimity" for upekkhā-vedanā (`sampayoga.md` L157, L181, L187), and the fifth-jhāna formula names the feeling. This is exactly the sort of collapsed distinction the repo's teaching mode tries to expose. The cetasika sheet also never explains why mettā and upekkhā are absent from the appamaññā heading: mettā is a mode of adosa, and brahmavihāra upekkhā is a mode of tatramajjhattatā ([CMA §7 Guide](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)).

## AGENTS.md inherits three overstatements and adds one

Apart from the votthapana claim above, `AGENTS.md` carries four further problems.

**Unprompted is "kammically stronger" (`AGENTS.md` L88; `citta.md` L15, L193).** None of the fetched sources ranks asaṅkhārika above sasaṅkhārika in kammic strength. Nārada describes the unprompted citta only as arising spontaneously ([Nārada I](https://budsas.org/ebud/abhisgho/abhis01.htm)). The closest support is Bodhi's remark that thīna-middha cannot arise in unprompted cittas, "which are naturally keen and active" ([CMA Ch. II](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). That speaks to keenness, not to weight of kammic result. The claim is a common teaching gloss and may well have commentarial backing in the Atthasālinī or CMA I §4 Guide, which were not checked. Until that check is made, both files should label it as tradition-level interpretation, not a sheet-level structural fact. `citta.md` L193 is the stronger version: it says "a spontaneous kindness and a spontaneous cruelty are both weightier" without qualification. `AGENTS.md`'s practical caveat ("Do not let the user read 'prompted is weaker' as 'don't bother'") is sound whichever way the strength question resolves.

**Lobha/dosa listed as "a specific rule" (`AGENTS.md` L50).** The exclusion is real, but the sources do not state it as a separate rule. It follows from the citta classification. Lobha is found "only in the eight types of consciousness accompanied by greed," dosa only in the two with aversion, and domanassa "accompanies only consciousness rooted in hatred" ([CMA Ch. I §4 Guide, Ch. II §13](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). No source says in so many words that lobha and dosa "never co-arise." In AGENTS.md's three grades, it belongs under a root-based partition inside the akusala class: closer to the first grade than to diṭṭhi/māna or karuṇā/muditā, which the texts address explicitly. `sampayoga.md` L173 gives the feeling-based derivation, which is sourced, but it also argues that "one citta with one object cannot be doing both," and that is the sheet's own inference. Similarly, L20 claims that ekālambana "does most of the work in the exclusivity rules," and L177 derives the issā-group exclusion from ekālambana. Both are interpretive. The sources give contrary modes for diṭṭhi/māna, contrary functions for doubt/adhimokkha, and "three cetanās" for virati ([Nārada II](https://budsas.net/ebud/abhisgho/abhis02.htm)).

**The wholesome/unwholesome partition is routed to the wrong section (`AGENTS.md` L45).** AGENTS.md cites `sampayoga.md`, "What Association Means," for the claim that wholesome and unwholesome cannot both be the present moment. That section (L13–24) establishes only that a citta and its factors are one event. The conclusion needs two more premises. First, one citta occurs per moment. Second, "fourteen are linked only with the unwholesome types, and the beautiful factors only with the beautiful types" ([CMA Ch. II §10](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). The principle is sound, but the cited section does not contain it.

**Paññā "uproots" moha (`AGENTS.md` L82, L87; `cetasika.md` L217).** Only supramundane path paññā eradicates. Paññā in a knowledge-accompanied sense-sphere citta displaces moha for that moment ([CMA §8](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). L87's "only paññā uproots" is fine as a contrast with mettā. As a practice target, though, L82 should specify insight culminating in path.

The antidote table also needs a grading note. "Issā → Muditā" rests on muditā's function, "being unenvious" ([CMA p. 90](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). That is functional opposition plus the class partition, not a pair-specific exclusion, and the Visuddhimagga's far enemy of muditā is arati (aversion or boredom), not issā ([van Gorkom ch. 33](https://www.vipassana.info/cetasikas35.html)). "Thīna-middha → viriya" is the sutta remedy, but in the jhāna-factor scheme CMA says the hindrance is overcome by **vitakka** ([CMA pp. 84–85](https://wiswo.org/books/_resources/zotero-attach/2007_A%20Comprehensive%20Manual%20of%20Abhidhamma.pdf)). Both pairings are defensible if labelled as functional opposition, which the AGENTS.md framework already allows for.

## Conclusion

The pattern behind the errors is informative. Every count was evidently copied from the manual, and the errors appear where the sheets explain why. The worst case is the one place where the repo turned an explanation into a practice instruction. Votthapana became "where kammic character is settled" because a practice framework needed one moment to aim at. The sources supply a better target than the sheet's version: wise attention (yoniso manasikāra), operating as the javanas arise, with votthapana as the inert moment just before. Moving the emphasis from the determining citta to attention keeps the "pause before reaction" advice and puts it on the factor the texts name as decisive. It also removes the impossibility of a rootless citta deciding "with greed."

A second lesson concerns the AGENTS.md grading scheme, which is sound. Its examples need one adjustment: lobha/dosa is a partition by root and feeling, not a stated pair rule. The ekālambana explanation that runs through `sampayoga.md` is useful, but it is the sheet's own theory of why the rules hold. It should be labelled that way, since AGENTS.md forbids presenting inference as a structural rule.
